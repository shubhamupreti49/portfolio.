Executive Summary

The Problem: Evidence on Nepal's Kamaiya, Haliya, Haruwa-Charuwa and Kamlari bonded-labour systems is scattered across journal articles, NGO and government reports, theses, laws and news, in English and Nepali, and some of it is disappearing from the web. Unbonded Archive brings it into one searchable place, records what each source studied and how, and links to the original.

Why It Exists

Nepal freed Kamaiya labourers in 2000 and Haliya labourers in 2008. What happened to them afterwards has been studied by many people in many places, but rarely in one place. With the archive, a researcher, journalist or student can find in an afternoon what would otherwise take weeks.

The archive complements my political-economy research on debt bondage in Nepal with Prof. Danish Khan.

Unbonded Archive, live at unbondedarchive.org

How Sources Are Added

Step What happens
Find Researchers search academic databases, university repositories and the publications of government bodies and organisations
Screen Each candidate is checked for relevance and duplicates, with the decision and reason recorded
Verify Title, authors, year and publication details come from the source or publisher, never from a search listing
Catalogue A researcher reads the source and records its population, place, sample, method and findings
Correct Every addition and correction is logged publicly with its reason

The catalogue is shared as an open dataset under CC BY 4.0, with citation metadata for the website and the data.

Limits

⚠️ Read with care: This is a curated collection, not a systematic review. Coverage is uneven across labour systems and languages, figures from different sources are not comparable, and the map shows where evidence exists, not how common bonded labour is.
