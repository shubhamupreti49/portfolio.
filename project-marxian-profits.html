Executive Summary

Core Contribution: Prof. Brennan's work explains where U.S. corporate profits come from by combining Kalecki's profit equation with Marx's rates of surplus and profit. I built an interactive R Shiny dashboard on the quarterly accounts, which now run through 2024 Q4, so readers of the paper can explore 61 years of the evidence themselves.

Research Background

Kalecki's insight is that, in aggregate, firms' profits are determined by spending. Rearranging the national accounts, gross profits equal the sum of the expenditures that realize them:

\[ \Pi = I + (X - M) + (G - T) + (C_k - S_w) \]

where \( I \) is gross private domestic investment, \( X - M \) the export surplus, \( G - T \) the government deficit, and \( C_k - S_w \) capitalists' consumption net of workers' saving. Prof. Brennan's earlier papers used this framework to show that, after the early 1980s, capitalists' consumption and household borrowing became the main sources of profit realization in the United States.

On the Marxian side, the dashboard tracks three ratios built from surplus \( S \), wages \( V \) and capital \( C \):

Ratio Formula What it measures
Rate of surplus \( S / V \) Profits relative to employee compensation
Rate of profit \( S / (C + V) \) Profits relative to total capital advanced
Organic composition \( C / V \) Capital intensity relative to wages

Brennan (2014), Cambridge Journal of Economics, covered 1964–2009; Brennan (2018), Review of Radical Political Economics, covered 1964–2013. The dashboard covers 1964–2024.

Engineering Notes

  • Wide-to-long reshaping: the source workbooks store one column per quarter; the app pivots them to long format and builds a continuous time index for plotting.
  • Fast start-up: parsed data are cached to an RDS file and rebuilt only when either source workbook changes.
  • Readable on any screen: light and dark themes, and recession bands drawn as full-height shapes so they stay correct when the y-axis rescales.
